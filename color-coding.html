The Color Coding System

Four colors, one rule: the cloth that touches a toilet never touches a desk. Color coding makes cross contamination control something you can see across the room.

Download Printable PDF

Why Color Coding Exists

Germs do not stay where they started; they ride on cloths, mop heads, and gloves. One red cloth used in the wrong place can carry restroom bacteria to a break room table. Color coding stops that with a system so simple it works on the busiest night: every color has a territory, and colors never cross the line.

Microfiber Cloths

ColorWhere It CleansNever Touches
Red / PinkToilets, urinals, and the surfaces right around themAnything outside the restroom
YellowRestroom sinks, counters, partitions, dispensers, and doorsToilets and urinals; anything outside the restroom
BlueGlass, mirrors, and polished surfacesRestroom fixtures
GreenDesks, tables, counters, dusting, and general surfacesRestrooms

Mops Follow the Same Logic

Mop ColorTerritory
Green or BlueCommon areas: lobbies, hallways, break rooms, offices
Red / PinkRestrooms only. Red pads and red buckets live in the restroom rotation and nowhere else.

Specialty Facilities Add Zones

Medical, clean room, and GMP food accounts extend the same idea with extra zone colors. The account's posted color map always wins; these are the common patterns:

ColorMedicalGMP / Food
OrangeTreatment roomsProduction & processing floor
YellowIsolation rooms (dedicated kit)Allergen control & segregated zones (dedicated kit)
Light BlueGlass & viewing panelsGlass & viewing panels
BlueBreak rooms & officesBreak rooms & offices

The Five Rules

  • One color, one territory. No exceptions, no borrowing when you run short.
  • Fold the cloth in quarters and rotate to a fresh face; 8 faces per cloth.
  • Retire the cloth when the zone is done or every face is used, whichever comes first.
  • Launder colors separately, and launder specialty zone colors (yellow zone kits) on their own.
  • When in doubt, throw it in the dirty bag. A clean cloth costs cents; a lost account costs everything.
